      Quick start

      Race dinosaurs by typing the passage on screen. Every correct key moves your racer closer to the finish.

      1. Save a nickname. On Race setup, enter a Racer nickname (up to 24 characters) and click Save player. Use Switch player if you already have a saved profile on this browser.
      2. Set starting pace. Take the pace test, enter a pace you already know, or play the red Challenge and choose Use as starting pace at the finish. In the pace test, your first keystroke starts the 60-second timer.
      3. Confirm Game Settings. Review rival speed, typing mode, and sound. Click OK — use these settings the first time. You can change them later.
      4. Pick a dinosaur. Keep your current racer or open More racers, pick a dinosaur to preview it, and click Accept runner.
      5. Hit the track. Pick a group in the Passages dropdown, choose an unlocked level, and click Start Level. Read the first-race reminder and choose OK, start race if it appears. Wait for 3–2–1 to finish before typing.

      Typing & controls

      Try the one-minute Challenge

      Save a racer nickname, then select the red Challenge button below Your starting pace. You don’t need to take the pace test first. Strict is the default: fix a red miss with Backspace before continuing. In Flow, misses still turn red but you can keep typing. Challenge remembers this choice for your racer on this browser. After the countdown, type as much of the mystery as you can in one minute. The finish shows words per minute and accuracy. Choose Use as starting pace if you want to set your pace from this run; Challenge never changes your level progress.

      Open Challenge scores after a run to post it under a public game nickname. This board is shared across computers when the online service is available, and is separate from regular race scores. No scores are posted without your action.

      What is the difference between Strict and Flow?

      In either mode, type the passage as shown, including capital letters, punctuation, and spaces. When the countdown ends, click the typing area if it doesn't have focus.

      • Strict: A wrong key marks the current character red and stops progress. Press Backspace once to clear it, then type the expected character. The mistake still counts, but extra keys pressed while the error is waiting for correction don't add more mistakes. Backspace doesn't rewind correctly typed text.
      • Flow: A wrong key marks the missed character red and moves the cursor on. Keep typing the next character. Backspace doesn't go back to repair previous characters.

      The pace test has its own mode selection. Set the mode for regular races separately under Game Settings.

      Pause, resume, restart, or leave a race
      • Press Esc or click Pause. Press Esc again or choose Resume when you're ready. Switching tabs or windows can pause a solo race.
      • Restart race in the pause menu restarts the current unfinished race from the beginning. It's different from spending a replay on a finished race.
      • Race setup (or the DR logo) takes you back to the Ready screen. Choose Resume race to return to a paused race. Opening Help also pauses an active solo race; it does not submit or restart it.
      Change rival speed, missed-key hints, sound, or colors
      • Computer difficulty: Move the slider down for slower rivals or up for faster ones. Projected rivals shows the expected WPM range. Change pace or Retake test lets you change your starting pace.
      • Missed-key helper: When enabled, it can bold letters you've repeatedly hit by mistake (four or more misses). It ignores whitespace and doesn't change your score. Disable it if you prefer plain text.
      • Key Noise: Turns typing feedback sounds on or off. The music note in the header separately controls race music.
      • Colors changes the color scheme. Aa toggles high contrast. ~ Reduce racer animation keeps the racers' poses steady while they advance; it doesn't change camera movement, menus, or fireworks.

      Scores, levels & replays

      Read the finish screen and review mistakes

      The big heading is your place in that race. WPM is speed (five correct characters count as a word), Avg WPM averages your recorded races in the level, and Peak WPM is the fastest measured pace in that race. Accuracy shows how many counted keystrokes were correct; Errors is the number of mistakes. No mistakes earns Perfect! A first-place race finish celebrates on the track even if you made a mistake.

      Open Mistake Stats to see missed keys, what you pressed instead, and where in the passage it happened. Switch between This race and All five races when available. From Ready, use Last race results to reopen the latest finish.

      Go to the next race or level and view the podium

      Up next shows the next race. Click Start Race on the finish screen or press Enter when that button is focused. A standard level has five races. After the fifth, choose Go to Level for the next level (if available) or View Podium to see the top three. You don't have to win to advance.

      Level points is separate from your individual finish. In a five-racer field first earns 5 points, second earns 4, and so on. The number of levels depends on the enabled passages in your chosen group; a 50-passage group provides ten levels.

      Replay a race or revisit a finished level
      1. On Ready, set Allowed replays per level. To share that limit across the game instead, turn on Override per level to per Game.
      2. On a finish screen, check the used and remaining replays, then click Use 1 Replay — Retry Race. A retry replaces the previous result for that race; it doesn't keep both scores.
      3. To revisit an older level, select the completed level on Ready and click Replay Level. This opens its existing results. Pick a Race there before spending a replay; opening the results alone doesn't spend one.
      Reset races for a passage group

      On Ready, choose the right group in Passages, then click Reset all races. Read the confirmation before proceeding. This clears matching local race progress and scores for that group, including matching race records in other profiles on this browser. It doesn't delete the passages or clear the online leaderboard. Export a backup first if you want to keep those results.

      Passage library

      Choose what to type or browse existing passages

      On Ready, the Passages dropdown chooses the group for your level. Change passages opens the library. Click a section title to expand or collapse it. Select a passage to read or edit it; Use selects its group, but the exact next passage comes from the level's race order. Finish the current level before changing groups if you want to keep playing that series.

      Write, edit, or copy a passage
      1. Open Change passages, then choose + New passage or Copy on an existing one.
      2. Enter a Section (existing or new), a Passage name, optional Source, and the Text. Use 30–5,000 characters after whitespace is cleaned up.
      3. Keep Enabled for races checked to include it. Use Preview to check how quotes, spaces, and punctuation will appear, then Save passage.
      Import or export a group as a text file

      Choose Import TXT and pick a plain-text file. In the simple format, the first nonblank line is the section name and each following nonblank line is one full passage. Keep each passage on a single line, even when it contains several sentences.

      Rainforest Practice
The muddy trail bends sharply by a waterfall.
At sunset, the youngest dinosaur finds a shortcut through the ferns.

      Export TXT makes a copy of the passage library you can import again. It doesn't include profiles, scores, or artwork. Some imports can replace matching passages, so keep an export before reorganizing a large library.

      Players, saves & backups

      Switch players and find a saved game

      On Ready, click Switch player and choose a saved name to load that player's progress. You can also enter a nickname in that window to save or switch. Use a nickname rather than a real name if you don't want it appearing on a shared score.

      Profiles, race history, and artwork are saved in this browser. A different browser, browser profile, device, private window, or site address can have a different set of saves. Clearing site data can remove local progress.

      Move all your saves to another computer
      1. On Race setup, expand Creator's Camp. Under Move to another computer, choose Export backup.
      2. Copy the private .dinobackup file to the other computer. Open the game there and close any other tabs running it.
      3. Back up the destination first if it already has saves. Choose Import backup, select the file, review the summary, and confirm.

      Import replaces the destination's local saves; it doesn't merge them. The backup includes profiles, progress, libraries, campaigns, and custom racer artwork. It doesn't change the online leaderboard. Keep the file private and use the HTTPS site or a localhost copy for transfers.

      Understand local and online leaderboard scores

      Open Leaderboard in the starting-pace card or on the finish page. On a site with online scores enabled, other players can see reported results, including a nickname, the passage title, speed, accuracy, and placement. The leaderboard does not share the full passage, your typing history, private saves, or custom artwork.

      Showing scores saved on this browser means you're looking at local scores. An offline or local copy may not have a shared leaderboard, but it can still save your own race results.

      Creator's Camp

      Build and play a campaign with Campaign Forge

      On Ready, expand Creator's Camp and open Campaign Forge. Choose Enable creator preview if asked. The preview is free; no payment or account is required.

      1. Click + Campaign, then add a title and optionally a goal, age range, and story notes.
      2. Fill five race slots per level. Choose passages and tracks, or leave the fallback options. Save before moving to another level. The campaign format supports up to ten levels.
      3. Use Use in Race Controls to select the campaign, then Play in Creator's Camp. Test level runs the current level; it requires all five slots enabled and replaces the currently active series, so finish any in-progress series first.
      4. Duplicate lets you try changes on a copy. Use Export and Import .dinocamp to move the campaign structure. Those files reference assets and passages; they don't include custom artwork, passage text, or a player's progress.

      Some editor fields (including reward and story notes) are part of the preview and don't create new in-game rewards by themselves.

      Make, align, and save a Custom Racer
      1. Open Custom Racer, then Choose artwork. A 1024 × 1024 transparent PNG works best; WebP and JPEG also work, up to 12 MB. Face right and leave room around tails and hats.
      2. For a transparent image pick Already transparent. For a solid hot-pink backdrop pick Remove hot pink. Raise the tolerance if the pink edge stays; lower it if parts of the character disappear.
      3. Use the faint default dinosaur and crosshair to line up the feet near X 512, Y 856. Adjust Size, Left / right, and Up / down, and check the Race-size preview.
      4. Name it and choose Save & use this Racer or Save & add another Racer. Under Saved custom Racers, choose Delete to remove artwork you no longer need.

      Custom artwork belongs to the active player on this browser. Make a backup to move it, or deliberately select it when hosting a local classroom room to share its portrait with the class.

      Use Typing DNA and understand Nemeses

      Open Typing DNA from Creator's Camp to see recent scores, correction history, and repeating mistake patterns. A Nemesis represents a pattern worth practicing; campaign boss passages can include those patterns. Clean encounters help master a weakness, while another miss on that pattern can reset its clean streak.

      For a parent or teacher, use Print tutor report. Your browser's print dialog can also save the report as a PDF if that option is available.

      Classroom races

      Host Race of the Day or join with a room code

      Local preview: These buttons only appear when your copy of the game supports classroom rooms. Try it in two tabs on the same computer, or use a shared address on a configured classroom network. A localhost address on one computer isn't shared with other computers.

      1. Teacher: In Creator's Camp, choose Host a race. Select an enabled passage and check the built-in and custom racers you want to share (1–32 racers, one student per racer). Choose Open room and share the four-digit code privately.
      2. Student: On the same game server, click Join race by the nickname field. Enter your nickname and code, click Join lobby, and choose an available dino.
      3. Start: The teacher clicks Start race after everyone has chosen. Wait for the shared countdown, then type in Strict mode. Positions show in the room standings as players finish.

      These races don't update solo progress or the public leaderboard. The teacher shares only the selected passage and portraits, and the room is temporary. If a code doesn't work, check that everyone is on the same server and that the teacher hasn't ended the room.

      FAQ & troubleshooting

      Why is everything dimmed on the Ready page?

      Save a nickname first. Next, set your starting pace with the test or the manual WPM option. Then review Game Settings and choose OK — use these settings. That unlocks racer and level selection.

      Why isn't typing moving my dinosaur?

      Wait for the countdown and check for a pause overlay. Click the typing area if it lost focus. If a red character is blocking the cursor in Strict mode, press Backspace and type the expected key. Check capitalization and spaces too.

      Why didn't I see fireworks for first place?

      Fireworks play on the track when you win a race and on the podium if you finish first overall in the level. They don't play on the score and stats page. Choose View Podium after the last race to see the level celebration. If your device's accessibility settings request reduced motion, you'll see still fireworks. The game's ~ Reduce racer animation toggle does not affect fireworks. On a winning podium, Play fireworks once or Replay fireworks plays the animation without using a race replay.

      Do I need an account or have to donate to play?

      No. Solo racing doesn't require an account or a payment. The Donate link is optional and opens a separate Stripe page. The game doesn't need a credit card to save your nickname. Dismiss any unrelated browser autofill suggestion in that field.

      Where did my progress go?

      Check Switch player for your saved nickname. Use the same browser profile and site address you played on before. If you changed computers or cleared your browser data, import a previously exported .dinobackup file. The online leaderboard isn't a copy of your saved game.

      Still stuck? Contact support

      Email Dino Type Racer support. Include the page you were on, what you clicked, what happened, and your browser and device. A screenshot can help; leave out private details, passwords, and payment information.
